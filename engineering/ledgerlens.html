LedgerLens

A bookkeeping cleanup and accountant handoff assistant designed around a simple principle: uncertain financial classifications should be routed to a defensible authority rather than hidden behind an overconfident model prediction.

Problem framing

The dangerous cases are the ambiguous ones.

Most monthly transaction cleanup is repetitive. The risk is in the minority of rows where the bank description does not contain enough business context to make a safe accounting decision.

A system that guesses confidently on ambiguous bookkeeping data can quietly propagate bad categories into financial statements, tax work, and month-end reporting. LedgerLens therefore separates fast deterministic handling from uncertain inference and from final human authority.

The product output is not a general ledger. It is a reviewed categorization package plus owner context that can be handed to a bookkeeper or accountant for substantive review.

Design objective

Automate what is defensible. Surface what is ambiguous. Preserve who or what authorized every finalized row.

System architecture

Deterministic layers first. Probabilistic reasoning later.

The current workflow places correction memory and deterministic rules ahead of fallback/model routing. Human review and audit are part of the architecture, not cleanup steps added afterward.

Trust boundary

Do not confuse model accuracy with workflow trust.

LedgerLens deliberately reports raw model behavior separately from the condition required before a row is considered safe enough to appear as finalized in the guided-demo ledger.

100%Finalized guided-demo rows procedurally verified before handoff
≈63%Reported raw model-only accuracy on the synthetic eval dataset
≈42%Reported raw model-only accuracy on the adversarial slice
3Defensible verification authorities: human review, correction memory, qualifying deterministic rule
What 100% means

It is a procedural guarantee about the workflow state, not a guarantee that every accounting or tax decision is substantively correct. Rows that still require review remain unresolved instead of being relabeled as verified.

Engineering decisions

Decisions that shape the safety model.

01

Rules and memory before AI

Cheap, traceable decisions run first. Model inference is reserved for cases the deterministic layers cannot settle safely.

02

Verification authority over confidence

A high model probability is not enough to finalize a row. Finalization requires a defensible decision authority.

03

Ambiguous vendors do not inherit blindly

Vendors such as Amazon, Costco, and Home Depot are intentionally blocked from unsafe fingerprint-memory generalization.

04

Audit the workflow, not just the output

Business, actor, request, action, and safe before/after metadata make the route to a decision inspectable.

05

Keep accounting boundaries explicit

The system produces categorization and handoff artifacts. It does not claim double-entry bookkeeping, reconciliation, tax filing, or CPA authority.

06

Public demo safety beats feature theater

The public site is synthetic-data-only and intentionally avoids claiming production authentication, tenant isolation, or safe real-bank-data handling.

Tradeoffs and limitations

Scope is part of the engineering.

Not accounting software

No double-entry ledger, accrual engine, bank reconciliation, tax handling, split transactions, or multi-currency accounting.

Not production multi-tenant SaaS

Business-scoped workflow foundations exist, but production authentication, tenant isolation, billing, retention automation, and disaster-recovery guarantees are not claimed.

No live accounting-system round trip

The current product exports reviewed artifacts. QuickBooks, Xero, Plaid, and Finicity integrations remain future work.

Human authority still has limits

The workflow can prove that a reviewer signed off. It cannot prove that the reviewer made the substantively correct accounting decision.
