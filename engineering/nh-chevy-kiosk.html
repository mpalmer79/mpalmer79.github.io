NH Chevy Showroom Kiosk

A touchscreen showroom assistant built around the fact that the customer is already physically inside the dealership. Inventory discovery, AI conversation, payment and trade workflows, deal worksheets, and staff escalation are designed as one in-store operating system rather than a web chatbot.

Problem framing

The customer is already standing in the showroom.

That single constraint changes the product. The system does not need to imitate a lead-generation chatbot. It needs to help a physically present customer explore inventory, understand options, prepare a deal, and reach a human employee at the right moment.

The kiosk combines customer-facing discovery with dealership operations. A conversation may move from a broad vehicle request into live inventory filtering, affordability checks, a trade workflow, an itemized worksheet, and finally a staff notification.

The architecture therefore separates conversational reasoning from the dealership facts and actions the assistant is allowed to use. Inventory remains read-only, tools define operational capabilities, and durable business artifacts are stored separately from short-lived conversation state.

Design principle

Use AI for conversation and orchestration. Keep inventory, calculations, state, and staff handoff behind explicit system boundaries.

System architecture

Conversation is only one layer of the system.

The touchscreen talks to FastAPI. FastAPI owns tool execution, state, retrieval, calculations, integrations, and durable workflow boundaries.

Engineering decisions

Optimize for dealership scale and showroom reality.

01

PBS XLSX as inventory source of truth

The nightly export matches an existing dealership operating path, avoids requiring vendor API provisioning, and keeps the kiosk read-only against the inventory system of record.

02

Tool use over RAG

Explicit tools let the model search, calculate, create worksheets and notify staff instead of stuffing a large inventory table into context and hoping the model reasons correctly.

03

TF-IDF over embedding infrastructure

At roughly dealership-sized inventory counts, sparse cosine retrieval is fast, local and cheap. External embedding latency and cost do not buy enough signal to justify the dependency.

04

Split transient and durable state

Live conversation state uses in-memory plus Redis. Worksheets, leads and outcomes go to Postgres because their retention and query patterns are fundamentally different.

05

Centralize model access

Claude calls flow through a constrained module, keeping tool behavior, fallbacks and a future model-provider swap from leaking across the entire application.

06

Human handoff is a product capability

Slack, SMS and email escalation are not ancillary integrations. They are the bridge from the kiosk workflow back into the dealership's real human operating process.

End-to-end behavior

A request becomes structured action.

The architecture document traces a representative request from natural language to inventory retrieval, structured tool results, rendered vehicle cards and persisted session state.

Example request

  • Customer asks for a blue Equinox under a budget.
  • FastAPI loads or creates the session state.
  • Claude receives explicit tool definitions.
  • The model requests inventory search with structured constraints.
  • TF-IDF retrieval ranks inventory and cheap filters apply color and price rules.
  • Structured results return to the model and frontend.

Operational continuation

  • Conversation state persists across turns.
  • Vehicle selections can become a worksheet.
  • Payment and affordability tools stay outside free-form model arithmetic.
  • Customer intent can generate a durable lead or outcome.
  • Staff can be paged through operational notification channels.

Tradeoffs and limitations

Production-shaped does not mean production-complete.

Vendor-data dependency

The kiosk depends on the freshness and shape of the PBS export. A spreadsheet path is practical, but it is still a batch integration with operational latency.

Single-model dependency

Claude is the conversational provider. Centralization reduces swap cost, but upstream availability and behavior still remain dependencies.

Development fallback is not production storage

The JSON fallback exists to make local demos easy. A production deployment is expected to configure its durable database explicitly.

AI does not own inventory truth

The model can orchestrate tools and explain results, but inventory facts come from the structured dealership data path, not model memory.
